# How to sound acid

This page is not about settings — [`06-the-knobs`](06-the-knobs.md) has those, and [`07-a-starting-patch`](07-a-starting-patch.md) has a patch to start from. This is
about the handful of phrases that make a bass line sound like *this* instrument rather than like a generic synth playing
low notes. They are habits, not values.

## Slide into an accent

The oldest trick in the book, and still the best. Put a **slide** on a step and an **accent** on the step it glides into:
the note bends up or down into a louder, brighter landing. That is the 303's whole vocabulary in two clicks.

Its cousin is the *rest*: an accent with nothing after it, followed by a rest, is what makes a line sound like it is
speaking rather than running. The step row makes slides that lead into a rest dim, with no `>` — that is the panel telling
you the glide has nowhere to go.

## Tune against the filter

A line written low with the cutoff low goes muddy; the same line an octave up with the cutoff low is fat and still clear.
So `TUNING` and `CUT OFF` are one decision made twice: if a phrase disappears when you drop it an octave, the answer is
usually to open the cutoff a little rather than to move the notes back up.

The same is true of the octave keys: `OCT +` an octave with the filter untouched is a brighter line, not just a higher one.

## A short decay and a lot of env mod

The pluck. Most of what people recognise as "acid" is the filter being kicked open on the attack of every note and shut
again inside a couple of tenths of a second. Long decay with a lot of env mod gives you a slow sweep — a different sound,
and a good one, but not this one.

Reach for **`DECAY`** first when a line sounds static, and **`ENV MOD`** first when it sounds dull. They are the two ends
of one gesture.

## The resonance is the voice

Turned well up, the filter starts ringing on whatever it is passing, and the instrument stops sounding like an
oscillator being filtered and starts sounding like a filter being played. That is the sound.

It also means the **cutoff has to be in the line's own register**. Resonance with the cutoff high is a whistle; the same
resonance with the cutoff down where the notes live is the throat. When a line screams unpleasantly, the fix is almost
always cut off down, not resonance down.

## One line, not chords

Sixteen steps, one voice. Write a phrase a bass player could play with one finger: a few notes, a couple of octave jumps,
a rest where you want the breath. Sparse lines carry the accent and the slide much better than busy ones; a step row full
of notes is a step row with nowhere to put emphasis.

A good test: hum the line. If you can, it will sound like a bass line. If you cannot, no patch will save it.

## Shuffle late, not early

Swing leans the offbeat back. A little of it — the knob just off zero — is what makes a straight sixteenth line feel like
it is being played rather than generated. Too much and the line limps; the groove has to stay a groove.

And it is a seasoning, not an ingredient: start straight, and push `SHUFFLE` up when the line needs a lift, rather than
writing the whole thing on a limping grid and hoping it settles.

## Three faults to listen for

- **It sounds thin.** Resonance is doing the work but the cutoff is too high — cut off down.
- **It sounds muddy.** The notes are low and the filter is low — open the cutoff, or `OCT +`.
- **It sounds flat, all one note.** Env mod or decay is doing nothing: one of them is at zero, and the accent isn't
  reaching the line.
