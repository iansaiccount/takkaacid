# What it is

takka-acid is a bass synthesizer built to do one thing: play a single acid line, very well.

It has **one voice** and **sixteen steps**. That is the whole machine. You write a line of up to sixteen notes, you shape
one sound with seven knobs, and the same voice plays both. Sixty-four patterns sit in **eight banks of eight** — `A1`
through `H8` — so a whole night of lines fits in one session.

## The four things that make the sound

Everything you hear comes from four places:

- **The wave.** A sawtooth is the classic acid sound: bright, buzzy, full of harmonics for the filter to bite.
- **The filter.** A resonant low-pass. Cutoff decides which harmonics survive; resonance decides how hard the filter
  rings at that edge. Most of the character of an acid line lives in these two.
- **The envelope.** It opens the filter on every note and falls away. Decay is how fast it falls; env mod is how far it
  opens. Short decay and a high env mod is the plucked, talking sound.
- **The accent.** A louder, brighter hit on the steps you mark. It is the emphasis the line leans on.

Slide is the fifth gesture but not a fifth knob: a step marked with a slide glides into the next one instead of
retriggering, which is where the 303's liquid phrasing comes from.

## What it is not

It is not a sampler, not a workstation, and not a plugin. There is no piano roll, no layers, no chords — one line at a
time, on purpose. If you want a polysynth or a drum machine, this is the wrong door.

What it does have: five demo songs to start from, sixty-four patterns to fill, MIDI in for a keyboard or a controller,
and a save file that keeps it all.
