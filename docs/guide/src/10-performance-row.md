# The performance row

The row between the knobs and the step keys is what you reach for **while the line is running**. Everything on it is safe
to touch mid-bar, and all of it can be heard without stopping.

## `OCT -` and `OCT +`

Transpose the whole pattern an octave down or up. This is not a temporary shift: the notes themselves are rewritten, so
`UNSAVED` comes on and a save keeps the new register. While the line runs, the steps you have not reached yet play in the
new octave; a step already triggered keeps the note it played.

Neither key stops at the edge of the range. Walk a line far enough up or down and its notes fall outside what the voice
can play: those steps go quiet, because a note the instrument cannot sound is a rest. `OCT -` the same number of times
brings them back.

## `SHUFFLE`

The swing knob. At zero the sixteenths are even; turned up, the pairs lean — the first of each pair long, the second
short, and the pair still exactly two steps, so the bar is always the same length. It is the one knob on the row you can
sweep and hear immediately, which is why it lives here rather than with the tone knobs.

## `RANDOM`, with its density and its scale

Two knobs and a key:

- **The left knob** prints a percentage: the **density**, one step in two by default. It decides how many of the sixteen
  steps sound.
- **The right knob** prints the **scale** it is on, from `CHROM` to `MAJ PEN`, and snaps to one of six faces like the wave
  switch does: chromatic, major, minor, dorian, minor pentatonic, major pentatonic.
- **`RANDOM`** fills the pattern with a fresh line drawn from those two settings.

What it keeps is the point: a reroll throws away the gates and the notes and **keeps each step's accent, slide and
velocity**, so the phrasing you put in survives while the tune changes underneath it. It also guarantees at least one step
sounds, even at zero density — a jump start that lands on silence is not a jump start.

Every roll is a brand-new line, so there is no undo. Save first if what you have is worth keeping.

## The row while the line runs

All three groups work with the transport running, and that is how they are meant to be used: octave the second bar, roll
a new line for the break, nudge the shuffle until the sixteenths sit where you want them.

`11-the-bank` is where the patterns live.
